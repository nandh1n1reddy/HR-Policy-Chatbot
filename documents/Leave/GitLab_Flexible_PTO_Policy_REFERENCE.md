## Flexible Paid Time Off (PTO) Policy — GitLab Inc.

**Source:** GitLab Inc., People Group Handbook
**URL:** https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/people-group/paid-time-off
**License:** Publicly published company handbook — reference use only, not public domain

### Key Policy Points
- GitLab uses an unlimited, flexible PTO model with no fixed annual day count, but team members are expected to take at least 25 days off per year to ensure they actually rest.
- A single vacation request is generally capped at 25 consecutive calendar days.
- The policy operates on a "no ask, must tell" basis: team members do not need manager permission to take time off, but must log it in the company's HR system (Workday) so others know they're out.
- All time away — PTO, public holidays, Family & Friends days, bereavement — must be logged so a manager is notified automatically.
- Once submitted, a PTO entry can be corrected or changed to a different category (e.g. switched to sick time) within 30 days of the original request.

### Sick Time and Related Leave
- Sick time is tracked separately from PTO; team members are not permitted to use standard PTO in place of sick leave.
- The sick time policy is meant to complement, not replace, any statutory sick leave entitlements in a team member's country of residence.
- Team members who respond to an on-call incident outside normal working hours are encouraged to take time the following day to recover, without it counting against their goodwill.

### Bereavement and Death Benefit
- In the event a team member passes away, GitLab pays a lump sum of $20,000 to a beneficiary of the team member's choosing (spouse, partner, family member, friend, or charity).
